# Part 1 — CERN

<div class="opacity-70">A 27 km circle on the French–Swiss border.</div>

---

# 🏛️ **What is CERN?**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-primary pad-tight">

### Founded **1954**

A post-war European project: 12 countries pooling resources to do science nobody could afford alone.

Today: **23 member states**, ~17,000 people from ~110 countries.

</div>

<div class="card card-secondary pad-tight">

### Where

Geneva, on the **Swiss–French border**.

The accelerator ring runs **100 m underground**, crossing the border four times.

</div>

<div class="card card-accent pad-tight">

### What we study

The **smallest things** in the universe — and the rules they obey.

Particles, forces, antimatter, the early universe.

</div>

<div class="card card-info pad-tight">

### Why a giant ring?

To see something tiny, you smash it hard. The bigger the ring, the harder you can smash.

</div>

</div>

# 🌀 **The Large Hadron Collider**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-primary pad-tight">

### Numbers

- **27 km** in circumference
- **−271 °C** — colder than outer space
- **13.6 TeV** — collision energy
- **40 million** collisions per second
- **~1 billion CHF / year** to run

</div>

<div class="card card-secondary pad-tight">

### What it actually does

Two beams of protons race in opposite directions at **99.9999991%** the speed of light, and we crash them into each other.

For a tiny instant, the collision point is hotter than the centre of the sun — like the universe **a billionth of a second after the Big Bang**.

</div>

</div>

# 🔬 **What CERN has found**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-primary pad-tight">

### **1983** — W and Z bosons

The carriers of the *weak force* — the reason the sun shines.

</div>

<div class="card card-secondary pad-tight">

### **1995** — Antihydrogen

The first atoms of antimatter, made and trapped.

</div>

<div class="card card-accent pad-tight">

### **2012** — Higgs boson

The piece of the Standard Model that explains why anything has mass.

</div>

<div class="card card-info pad-tight">

### **1989** — The World Wide Web

Tim Berners-Lee, working at CERN, invented it so physicists could share data.

You are reading this through his side project.

</div>

</div>

# 💡 **Why bother?**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-primary pad-tight">

### Because the universe is the experiment

Every other source of "what makes the world tick" is a model of this one. CERN is one of the few places we can **ask the universe directly**.

</div>

<div class="card card-secondary pad-tight">

### And because the side effects are huge

- The Web (CERN, 1989)
- MRI scanners (superconducting magnets)
- PET scans (antimatter physics)
- Touchscreens (capacitive R&D)
- Cancer therapy with proton beams

</div>

</div>

<div class="mt-md opacity-70 text-center">
"Useless" curiosity research has a strange habit of paying for itself ten times over.

# Part 2 — Quantum mechanics

<div class="opacity-70">The rules everything actually follows. Including you.</div>

---

# ⏳ **Where it came from**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-primary pad-tight">

### By 1900, physics was "done"

Newton's mechanics. Maxwell's electromagnetism. Thermodynamics. A young Max Planck was told **not** to study physics — there was nothing left to discover.

</div>

<div class="card card-secondary pad-tight">

### Then a few small problems wouldn't go away

- Why doesn't a hot poker glow infinitely bright?
- Why does light kick electrons out of metal in steps?
- Why are atoms stable at all?

Each "small problem" required **breaking** classical physics.

</div>

</div>

<!-- video candidate: Voyage_in_to_the_world_of_atoms.mp4 -->

---

# 🧱 **The classical picture**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-info pad-tight">

### What our intuition expects

- Things are either **particles** (little balls) or **waves** (ripples).
- A thing has a **definite** position and speed at all times.
- If I don't look, the world keeps doing what it was doing.
- Cause → effect, locally, no surprises.

</div>

<div class="card card-warning pad-tight">

### What the universe actually does

- Things are **neither** little balls nor classical waves.
- Position and speed are **not both** sharply defined.
- "Not looking" is not a neutral act.
- Distant things can be **correlated** in ways no classical signal can produce.

</div>

</div>

<div class="mt-md opacity-70 text-center">
This isn't philosophy. We've measured it.
</div>

---

# 🌊 **Wave-particle "duality"**

<div class="card card-primary pad-tight mt-md">

### The double-slit experiment

Send single electrons, one at a time, through two narrow slits and onto a screen.

You'd expect two stripes. You get an **interference pattern** — bright and dark bands, the signature of a wave.

But each electron lands as a single dot. **One** dot.

The pattern only appears after thousands of dots.

</div>

<!-- video later: double-slit animation / interference pattern build-up -->

<div class="mt-md opacity-70 text-center">
The electron didn't split. It didn't go through "both slits as a wave". It obeyed a rule that has no everyday analogue.
</div>

---

# 🎲 **Superposition**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-primary pad-tight">

### What it actually means

A quantum system can be described as a **sum of possibilities**, each with a complex-number weight (an *amplitude*).

When you measure, the probability of each outcome is the *square* of that amplitude.

</div>

<div class="card card-secondary pad-tight">

### What it doesn't mean

The system is **not** "secretly in one state and we just don't know."

Hidden-variable models that look classical have been **ruled out by experiment** (Bell tests, Nobel 2022).

</div>

</div>

---

# 🔗 **Entanglement**

<div class="card card-primary pad-tight mt-md">

### Two particles, one description

Prepare two particles together, separate them. Measuring one **instantly** determines the matching property of the other — even if they're light-years apart.

Einstein hated this. He called it *"spukhafte Fernwirkung"* — spooky action at a distance.

He thought it proved QM was incomplete.

**He was wrong.** Aspect, Clauser, Zeilinger — Nobel 2022 — closed the last loopholes.

</div>

# Part 3 — The myths

<div class="opacity-70">Where popular culture parts ways with the physics.</div>

---

# 🚫 **Myth #1 — "Quantum = mystical"**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-warning pad-tight">

### The claim

QM proves the universe is fundamentally mysterious, mind-driven, or spiritual.

"Quantum healing." "Quantum consciousness." "Quantum manifestation."

</div>

<div class="card card-success pad-tight">

### The reality

QM is a **mathematical theory** with the most precisely tested predictions in the history of science (one part in 10¹² for the electron's magnetic moment).

It's strange compared to your intuition. It is **not** strange to the equations.

If "quantum" is in the marketing copy, it's almost certainly not physics.

</div>

</div>

---

# 🚫 **Myth #2 — "The observer creates reality"**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-warning pad-tight">

### The claim

A conscious observer is required to "collapse" the wavefunction. Reality doesn't exist until you look.

</div>

<div class="card card-success pad-tight">

### The reality

A "measurement" in QM is **any irreversible interaction** with a larger system — a detector pixel, a gas molecule, a stray photon.

The pixel doesn't need a PhD. The pixel doesn't need to be conscious.

The Moon is there when nobody is looking.

</div>

</div>

<!-- video candidate: Cloud_Chamber_Audio.mp4 (a "measurement" caught on film) -->

---

# 🚫 **Myth #3 — "Schrödinger's cat is really alive *and* dead"**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-warning pad-tight">

### The claim

A cat in a box is in a literal superposition of alive and dead until someone opens the lid.

</div>

<div class="card card-success pad-tight">

### The reality

Schrödinger's cat was a **joke**. He invented it to ridicule the idea of extending superposition to macroscopic objects.

Cats are warm, wet, and constantly interacting with their environment. **Decoherence** picks an outcome in less than 10⁻²⁰ seconds — long before anyone opens the lid.

The cat is fine. Or not. But it's one or the other.

</div>

</div>

---

# 🚫 **Myth #4 — "Entanglement = faster-than-light"**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-warning pad-tight">

### The claim

Entangled particles can be used to send messages faster than light. Quantum internet means instant communication across the galaxy.

</div>

<div class="card card-success pad-tight">

### The reality

The correlations are real and faster-than-light. **Information** is not.

Each individual measurement looks like random noise. The correlation only appears when you compare the two records — and that comparison travels at light speed, on a normal channel.

**No-communication theorem.** It's a theorem, not a guess.

</div>

</div>

---

# 🚫 **Myth #5 — "Many worlds = parallel universes you can visit"**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-warning pad-tight">

### The claim

Every quantum event spawns a new universe where the other outcome happened. Somewhere there's a you who took the other job.

</div>

<div class="card card-success pad-tight">

### The reality

Many-worlds is **one interpretation** of QM among several — and a contested one. It's a way of avoiding "wavefunction collapse" by keeping all branches.

Even in many-worlds, the branches are **dynamically isolated** by decoherence. You can't visit them, signal them, or rob a bank in one.

The physics is the same in every interpretation. The interpretation is philosophy.

</div>

</div>

---

# 🚫 **Myth #6 — "Uncertainty = our equipment is bad"**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-warning pad-tight">

### The claim

Heisenberg's uncertainty principle is about clumsy measurements. With better tools we'll know position *and* momentum exactly.

</div>

<div class="card card-success pad-tight">

### The reality

Uncertainty is a **property of the system**, not the measurement.

A particle with a sharply defined position **does not have** a sharply defined momentum. Not "we don't know it" — *it isn't there to know.*

Δx · Δp ≥ ℏ / 2 is a theorem about what reality permits, not about what humans can build.

</div>

</div>

# Part 4 — What we actually know

<div class="opacity-70">A short, honest summary.</div>

---

# ✅ **Things that are settled**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-success pad-tight">

### Quantum mechanics works

Tested for **a hundred years**, every time, to absurd precision. Powers every transistor, laser, MRI, and LED you've ever used.

</div>

<div class="card card-success pad-tight">

### Local realism is dead

Bell tests (Nobel 2022) rule out classical "hidden variables" that act locally. The world is fundamentally non-classical.

</div>

<div class="card card-success pad-tight">

### Decoherence is the bridge

Big, warm, messy things look classical because they entangle with their environment in ~10⁻²⁰ s.

</div>

<div class="card card-success pad-tight">

### The Standard Model works

12 matter particles + 4 force carriers + the Higgs explain everything we see at CERN — to ~12 decimal places.

</div>

</div>

<!-- video candidate: sm.mov (Standard Model) -->

---

# ❓ **Things that are genuinely open**

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-info pad-tight">

### The interpretation problem

Copenhagen, many-worlds, pilot wave, QBism — they make the same predictions. We don't know which (if any) is "what's really happening".

</div>

<div class="card card-info pad-tight">

### Quantum gravity

QM and general relativity don't get along. We don't have a tested theory of how spacetime itself behaves quantum mechanically.

</div>

<div class="card card-info pad-tight">

### Dark matter, dark energy

95% of the universe is stuff the Standard Model doesn't describe. We see its effects. We don't know what it is.

</div>

<div class="card card-info pad-tight">

### Why these constants?

Why is the electron's mass *that* number? Nobody knows. The Standard Model has ~19 free parameters we just measure.

</div>

</div>

# How to spot a quantum-flavoured scam

<div class="grid grid-2 gap-md mt-md">

<div class="card card-warning pad-tight">

### Red flags 🚩

- "Quantum" + a wellness/finance/spirituality product
- "Observer effect" applied to your **mood**
- Entanglement + "instant communication" or "remote healing"
- Anyone selling you certainty

</div>

<div class="card card-success pad-tight">

### Green flags ✅

- A specific, falsifiable prediction
- Numbers, error bars, units
- "Here's where I might be wrong"
- A peer-reviewed paper you could in principle read

</div>

</div>
