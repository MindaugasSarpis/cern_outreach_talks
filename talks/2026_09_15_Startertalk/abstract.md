# Pentaquarks at LHCb: ten years of unanswered questions

Quantum chromodynamics allows colour-singlet hadrons of any quark content, yet for fifty years every baryon we knew was built from three quarks. That changed in 2015, when LHCb observed two structures in the J/ψ p spectrum of Λb⁰ → J/ψ p K⁻ decays that could only be explained by five-quark states. With nine times the data, the 2019 analysis resolved this into three narrow states, Pc(4312)⁺, Pc(4440)⁺ and Pc(4457)⁺, and the strange-quark partners followed in 2020 and 2022. Every one of them sits within about 20 MeV of a charmed-baryon–anticharmed-meson threshold, a coincidence that has shaped the theory debate ever since.

This talk walks through the discovery decade and then asks what these states
actually are. The leading candidates are hadronic molecules bound like a
deuteron, compact diquark configurations, hadrocharmonium, or no new state at
all but a kinematic effect at a threshold. Each picture makes distinct
predictions for spin-parity, widths, open-charm decays and partner states, and
most of these are amplitude-analysis observables in data LHCb already has. Some
of the channels with the strongest pentaquark predictions are only partially
reconstructed. The LHCb Vilnius group is working on methods to reconstruct the
missing particles in post-processing, using kinematic overconstraints. The
question of tighly bound vs. hadronic molecule pentaquarks remains one of the
focus areas in exotic hadron spectroscopy.
