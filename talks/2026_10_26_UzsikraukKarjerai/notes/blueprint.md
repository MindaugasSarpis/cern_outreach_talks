# Užsikrauk karjerai: blueprint (editor, 18 Oct 2026, run fe956c2ea202)

Overhaul of the 20-slide deck for flow and for grade 9–12 viewers. Lithuanian on screen and in the notes; everything else in English. Merged from three blueprints (one-question, the-particle, student-path), five critiques and four judges.

## Angle

One question carried by one particle. The cover asks it by about 0:30: „Kaip sužinoti, kad radai tai, ko dar niekas nematė?“ (how do you know you have found something nobody has seen?). The case is the five-quark particle that gathers on the cover: 51 years from the idea to finding it, with one false alarm on the way. The viewer is promised a turn at judging, and every part answers one step: what it is made of (3), where we look and how a mass becomes a dot (4–7), you judge a bump and it was chance (8), what enough data looks like (9–10), the cover's form acts out the search and the 2003 false alarm (11), the peak that held (12), the rule, said as the first answer (13), who looks and from where (14–15: the speaker's unplanned road, now Lithuania), with what (16–17: the open data), and the viewer inside the answer (18–19: a VU student saw the same bump in public data; the masterclass; you need not know now). The close answers the hook over the same form gathering again. Base: the one-question blueprint (all four judges' best); grafts: the-particle's history beat on the cover's form, its Bonn and coordinator hinge and its 'the same data' line; student-path's promise on the cover and its energy line on the tunnel.

## Arc

- Hook (1, 0.80 min): a particle that took 51 years, physicists fooled once, the question, and your turn.
- I. What we look for (2–3, 0.85 min): the web and the funnel; atoms, protons and neutrons, three quarks; five quarks is a pentaquark, which must be made.
- II. Where, and how a dot is made (4–7, 1.70 min): CERN and the tunnel (energy becomes new particles); one real collision (they break apart, the detector sees fragments); LHCb (one mass from the fragments, one dot).
- III. You judge; enough data (8–10, 1.75 min): you judge a 140-dot bump and it was chance; Ωc's five peaks with 59 453 dots; three quarks is usual, so are there particles of five.
- IV. The five-quark search and the rule (11–13, 2.45 min): the cover's form acts out 1964, the 2003 claim and its fall; 2015's peak held, 2019's three; 140 against 27 292 and the 1-in-3.5-million rule, said as the first answer.
- V. Who looks, and from where (14–15, 1.35 min): the speaker's road from 11th grade to a pentaquark search in Bonn; the same work now from Lithuania.
- VI. With what, and you (16–19, 2.20 min): 1 TB spheres, 800 TB open since 2023 (the data of the 2015 peak); over 4 000 TB open in front of over 100 000 TB; anyone can use it and a VU student did; the masterclass, theses, empty places, you need not know now.
- Close (20, 0.35 min): the form gathers again; found because of data and checks, and the data are open to you; „Ačiū“.
- Total: about 11.45 min of 12 (20 slides, 115 spoken words a minute plus each picture's unspoken seconds: clips, fills, flights, pauses).

## Takeaway

You find out something nobody has seen by collecting enough data, checking everything and not letting chance fool you. The data the pentaquark was first seen in are open to you now, and the way in starts at school. You do not need to know yet what you will become: notice what interests you and dig.
