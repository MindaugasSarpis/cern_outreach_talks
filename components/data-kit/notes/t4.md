## API

Fields: `pos name grains` are required. The source is one of `src` (d0.f32 by default, or
train-perceptron.json / train-ring.json) or `rare`. Then `x y` (`{col, log, range, ticks,
raw}`) or the shorthands `cols log range`, plus `where label size word wordY depth score
alpha selGain crowd cell lineAlpha boundaryGrains litDim thick seed ours oursTone readout`.
A readout proxy is `of` + `readout` with no source.

Steps (merged): `n`, `tip`, `color` ('none' | 'truth' | 'kept' | 'sel'), `box`, `line`,
`epoch` + `sweep` (+ `from`), `bend`, `show`, `lit`, `depth`, `undo`, `boundary: false`.

Readouts: `count rows kept right wrong accuracy signal signalKept bkgKept precision w1 w2
b loss epoch`, each with an optional `:role`.

The api: `value read values frame pointOf ours ready busy arm assemble`.

The README section has the full tables.

## Decisions

1. **Every boundary is a contour.** A box, a threshold, a line, a network and a score grid
   are all a "kept" function f(raw x, raw y). The boundary is f = 0, traced by marching
   squares (180 columns) and resampled into 1 800 grains by arc length. One mechanism
   draws them all, any boundary morphs into any other, and the colours and readouts use
   the same f. The alternative was a separate drawing per kind, with no morph between kinds.
2. **The real scatter uses log decay time on y, not decay time.** The frame runs 0.08-50 ps
   (fix pass): the data's own 0.150 ps start (a pre-selection in record 401, bake.md) then
   sits clearly above the floor, and 83 rows fall outside the frame instead of 179. On a linear 0-5 ps axis,
   60 000 of the 91 531 grains sit in the bottom tenth (TAU starts at 0.15 ps). On a log
   axis both classes spread over the frame, and log TAU is also the classifier's own input
   (bake.md's correction). The ticks are labelled in ps (0.2 … 20 ps). The unit sits in the
   top tick, so the one caption stays `log IPχ²`. **T4's notes must say that the y axis is
   logarithmic.** The alternative was linear ps up to 3, with 5 030 rows off the top.
3. **The bent boundary on record 401 is the two-input network** (`twoInputs`, log IPχ² and
   log TAU), evaluated from its logged weights at every contour point. It is not drawn
   from the grid, because the grid stops at 0.1-5 ps and is coarse near 0.15 ps on a log
   axis. This network's test accuracy is 0.613. The 0.629 on the slide belongs to the
   three-input network, which the 2D picture cannot show (bible risk: "two of the three
   inputs").
4. **Brightness follows density.** A pixel under n grains shows c·a·n^(1/2.2) (from the
   2.2 power in the fragment shader). So each grain's alpha is set to (8 / local density)^(1/2.2),
   counted over cells of 0.12 world units with a 3×3 blur and a bilinear read. A dense
   core then keeps one grain's colour: the crowd stays light blue and white stays white.
   This was tuned in four shots. With 0.22-unit cells, the τ = 0.15 ps ridge (5 % of the
   rows within 0.08 world units) burned white. With alpha flat, the white class bloomed.
   The white gain is 1.0 on a sparse set, 0.85 on a dense one and 1.15 on the rare set,
   whose white rows are also lifted to whole alpha × 1.25 (`whiteVis`) whatever the crowd
   around them. Tried in the fix pass: 1.8 bloomed into a grey disc; 1.3 with whiteVis 1.5
   bloomed again; 1.15 with whiteVis 1 left the whites grey and lost; 1.15 × 1.25 is clean.
5. **The boundary's alpha follows its length.** 1 800 grains on an 11-unit edge overlap
   about 12 deep. At a fixed alpha that bloomed into beads along the line (lab 4 slide 1,
   first shot).
6. **Labels and grains fade with camera distance.** Labels fade from 4 to 6 plot sizes
   away, grains from 8 to 12. Fixed-size tick numbers of another station stayed legible
   from 80 units, and 91 000 grains at 330 units became one bright point (lab 4, slides 9
   and 11). This is in my form only. The same holds for dk-hist and dk-heap, so it is filed
   as a request.
7. **Readout proxies** (`of`). The engine's `<StageCount for>` reads one `value()` per
   object name. A proxy object (`grains: 0`, no source) gives another scatter's `read(key)`,
   so the perceptron slide shows w₁, w₂ and b from one form.
8. **The rare set is built to the arithmetic.** There are 99 900 background rows (a seeded
   Gaussian cloud at 0.42 ± 0.1, 4 sd clear of the frame: at 2.3 sd the rejection showed
   as straight edges) and 100 signal rows (a tight cloud at 0.68 ± 0.06), and the cut u + v > c. c is placed
   so that exactly 999 background rows pass. The signal is then drawn by seeded rejection
   until 90 lie above the cut and 10 below. It is simulated, and the notes must say so.
9. **Tip-in.** The scatter takes `api.grains()` from the named dk-hist at the moment its
   tip step lands: positions, plus whiteness for T3's whitened peak. The grains then glide
   to their scatter places, bowed toward the camera and sweeping left to right. If the hist
   has already let its grains go (a slide opened on the tip step, or a scatter stepped more
   than 0.4 s after the hist), each row is stacked in its 2 MeV bin of the hist's
   `frame()`. That fallback has no P(signal) whitening.
10. **The perceptron threshold is 1.386, not the bake's 1.38564707.** The JSON stores the
    points rounded to 5 decimals, and the point that sets the threshold (1.38565) rounds
    above it. With 1.38564707 the count is 176. With 1.386 it is 177, as the bake says.
11. **The leak's depth axis.** z = M over 14 world units in the lab (9 by default). Mass
    runs right as seen from the plot's right side. The plane's own tick numbers step back
    while the axis is open, and the depth's ticks (1 845, 1 885, the label window) come in.
    **Fix pass:** at yaw 72 perspective spread the plot's 22-unit x extent across every mass
    band, so the leak read as a 3D blob. Now, while the axis opens, the plane squeezes to
    4 % of its width (`depth.squeeze`), its own axes fade (the y axis would stand through
    the middle band), the plane's thickness goes, and each grain's alpha follows the density
    seen from the side (the front-view density let the white band burn). The slide looks
    exactly side-on (yaw 90, pitch 4, dist 18.4). The labels moved out of the squeezed group
    into a sibling (`labG`), because a fixed-size sprite under a squeezed parent is squeezed
    too (the first side-view shot showed the depth tick numbers as dotted slivers).
    The undo slide flies back to the front view.
12. **Standardised axes (`std`).** The ring set is plotted in the units its network used
    ((raw − mean) / sd, both axes −3…3, ticks −2, 0, 2). Raw, it ran 2-8 against 0-60 and
    looked round only by the axis scaling. Lines, grids and readouts still take raw values
    (`ax.fwd` / `ax.inv`).
13. **Test points stand out (`dim`).** On the test step the 150 training grains dim to 0.2
    (`{show: 'all', dim: 'train'}`), so the 50 held-out grains are the only bright ones and
    the 4 wrong ones are the bright grains on the wrong side of the line.
14. **`axes: false`** for the rare set: its inputs are abstract, so no axis lines and no
    tick numbers (the first fix shot showed meaningless 0-1 ticks). A cloud and a cut.
15. **The stepper id of an unnamed plot** is `dk-scatter-<pos>` or a module counter, not
    `Math.random()`.

## Not verified

- **Motion of any kind.** Shots open each slide on its own and wait for `busy`, so I saw
  only end states. Not seen in motion: the tip from the hist (slide 6 used the fallback
  stacking, because the hist had already let go), the gather, the epoch sweep, the line
  bending into the ring, the test drop, the snap and the undo glide.
- The tip's main path, `api.grains()` from a hist still on screen, has not run. It needs a
  slide that clicks from the hist's whitened step to `{tip}` within one slide.
- Live stepping back and forth. By construction a back step is a 0.35 s jump to a state
  computed from the merged steps, with no latched uniforms.
- Tiers 2/3. Rows past the budget are thinned in the series order (our grain kept), and
  readouts count every row. Not rendered.
- `color: 'sel'`, `boundary: false`, `oursTone`, `wordY`, `reverse`, the score-grid path
  for a log axis (`space`), and `from` for a sweep. They follow the same code paths.

## Open

- **T4's real-rows slide shows 0.629, which is not the drawn boundary's number.** The
  boundary is the two-input network (0.613). The three-input 0.629 cannot be drawn in 2D.
  Either the slide says 0.613 next to this picture, or the notes say the picture is the
  two-input cut. bake.md already lists 0.6132.
- **Our grain (row 1) is class 1 in truth colours** (M 1 880.6 lies in the 1845-1885
  window), so it is drawn white. The bible wants its core light blue in T4. Set
  `oursTone: 'crowd'` for that, or leave it white and let dk-spark mark it. It lies on the
  background side of the honest boundary (log IPχ² 3.11), so it is misclassified, which
  T4 may want to say.
- 179 of the 91 531 rows fall outside the default frame (log IPχ² beyond −3.5…5, or decay
  time beyond 0.13-32 ps). They are counted but not drawn.
- The network boundary (slide 8) runs on through the empty 0.08-0.15 ps band down to the
  floor: that is where the two-input network puts its 0.5 contour, with no data under it.
  Honest, but T4 may prefer the boundary limited to where data is.
- The boundary chains reach the plot's own edges (slides 2-4): a line ends exactly at the
  frame. Seen at pitch 4, the top corners project about 12 px past the x axis's end,
  which the reviewer read as an overshoot. Not clipped further.
- 83 of the 91 531 rows fall outside the 0.08-50 ps × −3.5…5 frame (counted, not drawn);
  the long-lived arm still reaches the frame's top right corner.
- The squeeze and the side-view alpha are seen only at rest (slide 9). The fold itself,
  the plane narrowing while the camera swings to yaw 90 and the grains snap apart, has
  not been seen in motion, nor the undo unfolding.
- The sparse sets read best small and close (6.5 units at dist 10.5). That is a camera
  choice for T4, not a kit constant.
- In the lab the stations had to sit apart (z ±150) so that other stations were out of
  view. The distance fade covers this for dk-scatter but not for dk-hist or dk-heap (see
  the requests).
